# Biological interpretation and evidence audit

**Scope.** This report audits existing checked-in visualizations, derived tables, analysis notes, and their generating code. No enrichment, plot regeneration, broad re-analysis, or remote dataset retrieval was performed. Counts below refer to the artifact-specific cohorts stated in context; candidate results are from a sampled UMAP population, not all 244,046 intervals.

## 1. Executive summary

The local artifacts support descriptive statements about sequence composition, raw Carbon-3B likelihood, exact-sequence copy counts, taxonomy strings, and embedding neighborhoods. They do not establish gene function, biological novelty, adaptation, pathogenicity, fitness, or evolutionary distance.

The strongest candidate-level observation is that a DBSCAN region in the **raw 2-D UMAP** (`eps=1.0`, `min_samples=20`) contains 3,501/20,000 projected intervals (3,419 labelled Fungi, 82 Metazoa). Within the sampled Fungi, literal lineage components include `Ascomycota` for 2,861/3,419 candidates (83.68%) versus 6,748/10,487 comparison intervals (64.35%); `Basidiomycota` is less represented (14.92% versus 30.14%). These are sample composition contrasts, not evidence that a fungal interval had a higher probability of selection: the sample is not the full cohort, and the lineage fields are ordered components rather than rank-annotated taxonomy.

The candidate fungi also have higher raw mean log probability than the sampled noncandidate fungi. The record-clustered estimate is +1.524 nats per supervised token (95% CI +1.471 to +1.578), reducing to +1.310 after GC and depth-4 component adjustment. It remains +1.293 in the depth-6 common-support sensitivity. This is an in-sample association after limited measured adjustment, not independent validation: candidate membership was discovered from embeddings/UMAP in the same sample; length, coding status, and gene type were unavailable for this candidate join; and source records may not be independent genomes. The score is not Markov-corrected.

The whole common-cohort likelihood analyses show modest unadjusted rank association with GC (Spearman ρ=0.228), weak association with entropy (ρ=−0.068), and weak association with length (ρ=0.074). Exact-sequence repeats have higher raw-score medians than singleton rows, but exact repeats are uncommon in the candidate fungi (4/3,419). No composition-only or Markov-adjusted baseline is present. Thus likelihood and embedding patterns cannot be interpreted as function, novelty, or biological importance.

The kNN graph is strongly lineage-prefix-consistent at shallow/intermediate depths, above a fixed-graph, abundance/degree-aware permutation null in the supplied audit. This is descriptive taxonomy structure, not held-out classification or phylogenetic validation; exact-sequence-deduplicated agreement cannot be calculated. Published and current-taxonomy agreement disagree at depths 9 and 10. Embeddings are highly anisotropic, and raw versus whitened UMAPs are separate fitted projections, so visible islands are projection-sensitive.

**Hugging Face streaming is not justified now.** It would not solve the missing annotations or provide indexed random access. First obtain locally the minimal interval-keyed CPU metadata/hash table for already selected candidates and controls. Retrieve sequence strings or external annotations only for the eventual small, explicit case-study set if a functional claim is pursued.

## 4. Biological findings

### Taxonomic composition

The candidate case study has 3,419 fungal and 82 metazoan intervals among 3,501 members; its 16,499 comparison intervals include 10,487 Fungi and 6,012 Metazoa. These are the 20,000 projection sample, not the whole 244,046-key cohort. Within sampled Fungi, Ascomycota-labelled components are frequent and Basidiomycota-labelled components less frequent in the candidate. Sordariomycetes- and Eurotiomycetes-labelled components are more common, while Agaricomycetes is less common. Literal components labelled Aspergillus, Colletotrichum and Penicillium are more frequent; Suillus is less frequent. These counts and proportions can be reported descriptively.

The taxonomy join is one lineage string per interval, not separate rank columns. Components at the same depth can represent different formal ranks, and missing depth can mean an omitted intermediate component or truncated taxonomy. In particular, do not report tested “phylum/class/family/genus/species” coverage based on these component positions. Exact full lineage paths are available and safer to quote. Candidate-vs-comparator share describes composition; the within-group candidate rate requires the number of all eligible intervals from that taxon and a clearly defined full-cohort selection indicator. Candidate membership here is limited to a UMAP sample, so it does not estimate population selection propensity.

### Sequence characteristics and likelihood

Full-cohort tables and likelihood plots support descriptions of sequence length, GC, entropy, gene type/coding flag, and score distributions. GC has a modest positive unadjusted score association; entropy and length have weak global rank associations. Recorded gene classes differ substantially in raw-score medians, but unequal group sizes and sequence length/composition/tokenization confound interpretation. These comparisons do not isolate biology from model behavior.

Candidate-level joins provide GC and entropy values for all 20,000 sampled intervals and candidate score for all 20,000. GC distributions overlap across all fungal GC deciles, and GC-standardized likelihood difference is +1.413 nats/token; broad component-adjusted regression estimates remain positive. This makes a pure GC-only explanation less consistent with the data, but it does not control unavailable length, coding status, gene type, source-genome dependence, or post-selection. No Markov/composition baseline is present. Candidate sample does not have local length, strand, coding or gene-type values, despite such features existing in full-cohort artifacts.

### Embedding geometry and neighbors

An extreme variance channel is directly observed, making geometry choices consequential. Raw and whitened UMAPs use separate projections and are exploratory. The trinucleotide-vs-embedding PCA comparison is visual only and does not quantify the dependence between learned embedding and sequence composition. kNN lineage agreement is strong at shallow/intermediate depths and exceeds the audit's graph-fixed, degree/lineage-depth-aware label null. However, the agreement statistic is edge-weighted, eligible edge sets shrink with depth, published/current taxonomy differ at depths 9 and 10, and exact sequence deduplication cannot be performed without interval-level hashes. This supports taxonomy-associated representation neighborhoods in this cohort, not phylogeny, function, or generalization.

### Duplication and dependence

Common-cohort duplicate counts represent SHA-256 identity of strand-normalized sequence. Repeated rows have much higher raw likelihood medians, indicating redundancy is a score correlate. Candidate fungi have only four repeat-marked intervals; removing repeat-marked rows leaves the score difference materially similar in existing analysis. However, exact hashes are unavailable in the candidate table, so exact one-row-per-sequence estimates, taxonomy conflicts per sequence, near-duplicate analysis, and interval-overlap checks remain unavailable. Interval counts, unique sequence hashes, source records, accessions, and genomes are distinct units and must not be conflated. Candidate counts span 912 source record IDs for Fungi versus 1,663 comparison source record IDs; those IDs are not verified independent genomes.

## 5. Candidate case studies

Use the raw UMAP DBSCAN component `raw_C2_e1_m20` as a **positive descriptive case study**, explicitly called a projection-defined region. Report its 3,501/20,000 membership, 3,419 Fungi, the lineage-component contrasts above, the score association with adjustment caveats, and the fact that it does not persist as a distinct component in the separately fit whitened UMAP. Do not call it a biological cluster.

Use the candidate-vs-comparator fungal sample as a **negative/contrast case study**: Basidiomycota- and Agaricomycetes-labelled components are less represented, and the literal Suillus component is 122/3,419 (3.57%) versus 930/10,487 (8.87%). The same unranked-lineage and sample-selection qualifications apply. This guards against presenting only enriched-looking groups.

Do not nominate individual interval identifiers from this audit. The report's defensible units are group summaries; a future sequence-level case set should be selected from the existing candidate/control membership table after adding length, gene type, coding status, source identity and sequence/hash access, and should include both high and low model-score examples matched on relevant covariates.

## 7. Minimal next-step plan

1. **Freeze and document the candidate definition and target population.** If the target is `raw_C2_e1_m20`, label it a post hoc, sample-specific exploratory region. For any rate claim, apply a prespecified reproducible selection rule to the defined full cohort or an independent held-out cohort.
2. **Make one small, local keyed join for candidates and controls** (or full common cohort only if necessary): canonical interval key; separately retained source record/genome identity; candidate flag; length, GC/entropy, coding/gene type, strand, exact sequence hash; explicit-rank taxonomy and missingness. Verify one-to-one cardinality and record all unmatched/ambiguous rows. Recompute within-taxon candidate rates, effect sizes/CIs, and a small prespecified set of lineage comparisons with multiplicity correction, clustering uncertainty by source/genome and a sensitivity collapsed by hash. Do not test every lineage depth as independent evidence.
3. **One held-out or independent validation** of candidate likelihood/composition/embedding relationships, matching or adjusting at minimum for length, GC, gene class and lineage. If no independent cohort exists, present the current estimates as exploratory only. A likelihood contrast is not validation if likelihood was used to select candidates; here candidate selection is embedding-based, but the same-sample discovery still creates selection bias.
4. **Retrieve sequence and external annotations only for a small, explicitly selected case set** if the results section is to make gene, novelty, or function claims. Preserve source provenance; use reference matches and gene/domain annotations and state database versions. No annotations should be inferred from taxonomy.
5. **For neighborhood claims only if retained in the writeup:** recover the original taxonomy snapshot and interval-to-hash map, then reproduce depths 9–10 and evaluate a deduplicated graph. Current evidence is adequate for a qualified descriptive result without that follow-up.

### Hugging Face retrieval decision

Do **not** stream Hugging Face datasets for this stage. Existing HTML/CSV/metadata provide the descriptive findings, and the missing candidate covariates should first be sought in local derived outputs or the project's existing cached CPU table. The actual sequence and external annotation are needed only for a named, small case set and can be retrieved by verified interval/source identifiers if an indexed source is available. If streaming later becomes necessary, define the exact keys/columns first: streaming is sequential and does not imply random indexed lookup; record dataset revision, rows scanned/retrieved, and join cardinality. Do not redownload embeddings or likelihoods already represented locally.

## 8. Suggested final-writeup structure

### Results outline

1. **Cohort and feature coverage:** common-cohort key definition, sample sizes, missingness, and distinction between interval, exact sequence, source record and genome.
2. **Descriptive sequence and score variation:** length/GC/entropy distributions, raw likelihood correlations, and gene-class summaries with confounding caveat.
3. **Embedding neighborhoods:** anisotropy, qualified lineage-prefix agreement, and explicit null/denominator details; no function/evolution language.
4. **Exploratory projection case study:** define the raw UMAP/DBSCAN region, sample-specific taxonomy-component composition, GC/score associations, negative contrasts, and projection/selection caveats.
5. **Limitations and validation needs:** rank ambiguity, sequence-level annotation absence, cohort selection/dependence, and no functional validation.

### Defensible claims that can be written now

- The common cohort contains 244,046 unique interval keys; embedding plots use a deterministic 20,000-point sample.
- In the sampled raw UMAP, the specified DBSCAN region contains 3,501 intervals, 3,419 of which are labelled Fungi.
- Literal fungal lineage components differ in candidate/comparison composition, including higher Ascomycota-labelled share and lower Basidiomycota-/Agaricomycetes-/Suillus-labelled share in the candidate sample; these are not named-rank selection effects.
- Raw mean log probability has modest unadjusted association with GC and weak association with entropy/length across the common cohort; exact repeated sequences have higher score medians.
- The candidate fungal score association remains after the available GC and broad component adjustments, but it is exploratory and not fully confounder-adjusted or independently validated.
- The embedding neighborhood graph has lineage-prefix structure above the specified abundance/degree null, subject to taxonomy-depth mismatch and missing deduplication.
- Current results do not establish function, novelty, pathogenicity, adaptation or importance.

## 9. Limitations

- **Representativeness:** the 244,046 interval-key common cohort and 20,000 projected sample are not shown to represent the full 32.4M-row source or genomic diversity. Candidate results specifically use the 20,000 projection sample.
- **Taxonomic coverage:** only ordered lineage strings are locally available in the candidate join. Components are not rank-safe; ambiguous, omitted and truncated terms cannot always be distinguished. The requested taxa are verified only as literal components where present, not all as formal ranks.
- **Selection bias:** the region is derived from raw two-dimensional embedding UMAP and DBSCAN and assessed in the same sample. It is exploratory even though the candidate rule itself did not directly threshold likelihood. Embedding/color inspection can still motivate post hoc selection.
- **Confounding:** candidate-level length, coding status, strand and gene_type are absent. Existing GC/lineage adjustment is incomplete. Raw Carbon likelihood reflects model/tokenizer behavior and composition as well as any biological regularity.
- **Multiplicity:** many lineage components and UMAP views were available; current candidate taxon shares are descriptive, not multiplicity-adjusted tests. Nested taxonomic components are dependent.
- **Dependence:** multiple intervals may share source records, genomes, exact sequences, overlapping loci or assemblies. `record_id` is not established as an independent-genome identifier. Edge-level kNN measurements share queries/neighbors; query bootstrap does not eliminate all graph dependence.
- **Duplication:** exact identity counts are available, but hash-to-key mapping and one-per-sequence analysis are not. Exact identity does not detect homology or near duplicates.
- **Embedding interpretation:** extreme channel variance, PCA/whitening choices, separate UMAP fits and sampling can affect geometry. Geometric proximity is not functional similarity.
- **No functional validation:** no sequence-level homology, gene/domain annotation, reference-database comparison or experimental result is part of the audited artifacts.
- **Taxonomy version discrepancy:** published and current lineage tables agree through prefix depth 8 but differ at depths 9 and 10; deeper neighborhood results need the original snapshot for exact reproduction.
